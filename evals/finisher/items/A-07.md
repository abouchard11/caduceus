**Question:** Should a solo technical founder open-source an infrastructure tool or keep it closed and sell it?

## Recommendation (weighted, not voted)

**Lead seat:** FORGE (weight 0.350 = believability x confidence 0.70)

Keep it closed-source with a free tier or trial, validate that buyers will pay with 3-5 real conversations, then decide on open-sourcing based on whether distribution (not sales) is the binding constraint. If the operator's goal is credibility/hiring rather than cash-flow, open-source it as a portfolio asset — but that's a different goal.

**Why:**

- Open-sourcing is reputationally near-impossible to reverse (Draft C's one-way-door argument with real evidence: Elastic, Redis, HashiCorp all retreated with community backlash). The default should favor the reversible choice.
- The community that OSS is supposed to generate is itself a full-time job (triage, docs, governance, security, PR review) that a solo founder cannot fund and should not assume will appear spontaneously.
- The brief withholds deciding facts: is there inbound demand, is the tool a commodity, what's the runway, is there a compliance-sensitive buyer segment. Without these, any recommendation is a guess.
- Closed-source doesn't mean no community: free tiers, public docs, transparent roadmaps provide adoption benefits without opening the repo.
- If the operator's real goal is credibility/hiring/acquihire (which Alex's context suggests), then OSS functions as a portfolio asset and the 'sell vs. open' framing is the wrong question.

**Known risks in this recommendation:**

- If the tool is a commodity in a crowded OSS category, closed-source faces real adoption friction that open-core sidesteps.
- If there's already inbound demand and willingness-to-pay is proven, the maintenance burden argument weakens.
- If the operator's actual goal is not cash-flow but credibility/hiring, the closed-first default is wrong for his specific situation.
- The 'free tier' middle path may not provide enough distribution to overcome the unknown-solo-founder adoption friction.
